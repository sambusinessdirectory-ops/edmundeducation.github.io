| 2401 | A director used silence to “tighten the screws” of suspense. | [Cambridge English Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/put-tighten-the-screws-on) defines the idiom as applying pressure to **someone** to make that person act. The source's suspense use appears to extend that core sense; the dictionary entry does not establish it as a standard teaching model. | Keep `hold-editorial`. If a lesson is later written, teach the conventional interpersonal-pressure meaning first and review the cinematic extension separately. |
| 2291, 2396 | A noun “a slow burn” and adjectival “slow-burn tension”. | [Cambridge English Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/slow-burn) records noun senses involving gradually developing anger and a low-activity period, with an example of a gradually unfolding series. This does not by itself prove that the noun and adjective should share one target matcher. | Keep 2396 on hold until the prospective 2291 lesson explicitly covers the adjectival construction or editorial review justifies a separate target. |
