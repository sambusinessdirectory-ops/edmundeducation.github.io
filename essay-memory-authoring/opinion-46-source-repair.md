# Opinion 46 source reconciliation

## Source issue

The public question asks whether money spent by governments and individuals on animals and habitats would be better spent on poverty and health care. The previous essay answered a different question about the duties of richer and poorer countries. That mismatch made its model answer, translations, exercises, and narration unsuitable for the public prompt.

## Decision

Keep the public question. Replace the essay with a four-paragraph, 17-sentence IELTS Task 2 answer that takes a **largely disagree** position. The response recognises the urgency of poverty relief and health care while rejecting a general transfer of conservation funds.

- The introduction states the competing spending claim and the author's qualified position.
- Body paragraph 1 explains how habitats can support clean water, food production, flood protection, and human health. A wetland example connects wildlife and nearby homes/clinics. These are presented as possible benefits, without invented numerical claims.
- Body paragraph 2 rejects treating social welfare and conservation as automatic rivals. It gives examples of conservation work that can pay local people, and includes a clear exception: wasteful schemes should be improved or replaced, with savings used for urgent human services.
- The conclusion addresses both public budgets and private donations, matching the question's reference to governments and individuals.
