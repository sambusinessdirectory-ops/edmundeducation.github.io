# Native English course redesign brief

## Scope

Redesign the approved lessons numbered 7–549 as one reviewed curriculum release. The 17 lessons in `IMPORT-REVIEW.md` remain visible only according to their current release status; do not publish incorrect source material. Lesson 102 is the quality reference, not a template to copy.

## Variety rule

The fourteen activity styles below form **one shared pool across the whole catalogue**. Each lesson uses **five or six distinct styles** chosen for its language point and source situation. All fourteen recur across the 400+ lessons; no lesson is required to use all fourteen. Vary order, question count, interaction, context, and page composition. Adjacent lessons must not share a recognisable sequence. A style counts only when it tests a distinct ability, not when the same answer is presented in another visual wrapper.

| Style | Learner action | Evidence of learning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scene decision | Respond to a concrete situation | Chooses a fitting expression, not just a familiar phrase |
| Tone comparison | Distinguish degrees of feeling or politeness | Explains why a close alternative changes the tone |
| Audio inference | Listen before seeing text | Infers intent or situation from speech |
| Dialogue branch | Choose what to say next | Continues a believable exchange |
| Reverse match | Match expression to a situation | Knows when it applies, not merely what it translates to |
| Near-miss repair | Improve an understandable but unnatural line | Identifies the actual usage difference |
| Meaning contrast | Compare two natural expressions | Separates related but distinct needs |
| Detail hunt | Extract one necessary detail from speech or text | Answers on evidence rather than recognition |
| Message rewrite | Adapt wording for audience and purpose | Changes register while keeping meaning |
| Conversation continuation | Add a useful detail or follow-up | Keeps the interaction moving naturally |
| Instant spoken reply | Respond after an audio cue and pause | Produces language before hearing a model |
| Situation change | Transfer the expression to a new setting | Applies the underlying idea rather than copying |
| Explain your choice | Identify the contextual clue behind an answer | Shows why the answer fits |
| Open final challenge | Produce a response with a concrete supporting detail | Uses the lesson independently |

## Non-negotiable checks

- No target answer, transcript, or answer-bearing hint appears before the relevant attempt. Reveal explanations and transcripts afterward.
- Do not mark another natural answer wrong merely to force the lesson's target phrase. Narrow the context, accept alternatives, or ask about the nuance instead.
- Distractors must be plausible and differ in meaning, tone, register, or situation. Feedback explains that difference.
- Repeatedly filling the same word or copying the target sentence does not count as a distinct activity.
- Audio and written turns must agree; two-speaker dialogue uses two voices and matching visual speaker treatment.
- New questions use new IDs and a new lesson revision, so old progress cannot complete a redesigned lesson.
- Audit the complete catalogue for style distribution, neighbouring lesson similarity, answer-position bias, answer leakage, ambiguity, and missing audio before one batch release.
- Check that every redesigned lesson uses exactly five or six distinct styles and that all fourteen styles are represented across the approved catalogue.
