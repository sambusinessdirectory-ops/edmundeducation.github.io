Tom Yengi 訪問

Adelaide Library News article

Interview with Tom Yengi

by Katy Milanovic (Assistant Librarian, Adelaide Library)
24 November 2025

For those of you who haven’t ever experienced a People Library…think of a hall full of really interesting storytellers. Each on their own little platform, telling the story of their daily lives. I can still remember the first People Library event I went to in 2018. The storytellers were all street cleaners here in Adelaide. I loved how we could ask questions – I learnt so much. That’s the great thing about People Library events – you can ask questions and really engage with the speakers.

如果你從未體驗過真人圖書館，可以想像一個大堂內有很多有趣的說書人，每人站在小平台上講述日常生活。我仍記得 2018 年首次參加真人圖書館，說書人都是阿德萊德的街道清潔工。我很喜歡可以提問，學到很多；活動的優點正是可以提問並真正與講者交流。

So, I was excited when I heard that Tom Yengi, one of the founders of the People Library, was visiting our library here in Adelaide this week. We made some time to have a chat.

得知真人圖書館其中一位創辦人 Tom Yengi 本星期到訪阿德萊德圖書館，我十分興奮，並抽空與他訪談。
Katy:

So, Tom, we have many regulars at our People Library events here in Adelaide every month and we know they’ll be interested to hear from you. So how did you first get the idea to do a People Library event?

Tom，我們每月的活動有很多常客，他們一定想聽你的分享。你最初怎樣想到舉辦真人圖書館？
Tom:

Well, I don’t know if you remember but a lot of refugees arrived in Australia in the early 2000s. Some people weren’t very welcoming towards them…

2000 年代初有很多難民抵達澳洲，不知道你是否記得。有些人並不歡迎他們……
Katy:

I was a bit young to really remember, but I’ve read about it.

當時我太年輕，不太記得，但讀過相關資料。
Tom:

Right. Well, I wanted to see if I could tackle people’s prejudices by giving them the chance to hear more about the refugees’ lives.

我想讓人們有機會多了解難民生活，看看能否藉此處理偏見。
Katy:

So that was your main motivation?

那就是你的主要動機？
Tom:

Yeah. You know, prejudice can easily arise when groups of people don’t know much about each other.

是的。群體彼此了解不多時，很容易產生偏見。
Katy:

I know what you mean.

我明白你的意思。
Tom:

Yeah, so a couple of us planned an event at our library in Darwin, where people could come along and hear more from the refugees themselves. That was in 2002.

於是我們幾個人在達爾文的圖書館策劃活動，讓人們親自聽難民分享。那是 2002 年。
Katy:

Well, it’s certainly something that you yourself must be immensely proud of. And what about you nowadays? What do you do now?

你一定非常自豪。那你現在做甚麼工作？
Tom:

Well, I’ve just been appointed to be the librarian on the Pitcairn Islands in the Pacific Ocean. It’s the most remote library in the world.

我剛獲委任為太平洋皮特凱恩群島的圖書館員，那是世界上最偏遠的圖書館。
Katy:

Sounds like a big adventure! Well, thanks so much for coming in today, Tom. It’s been lovely to talk to you.

聽起來是一場大冒險！Tom，謝謝你今天到來，很高興與你交談。
