Newspaper Clipping from the Hong Kong Newspaper

Lucky Escape for Hong Kong Mountaineer Inspires a New Direction

Kathmandu, 14th October 1980

Hong Kong resident Pris Fong had a close call last week when climbing Manaslu mountain. Recovering after her rescue, the lucky lady said that her adventuring days may be over.

Manaslu, meaning ‘mountain of the spirit’, is located in the Nepalese Himalayas. Standing at 8,163m above sea level, it is the eighth highest mountain in the world. First climbed in 1956, the mountain has developed a reputation as a place of great danger.

Injured during the climb, Ms Fong believes that the experience has given her the inspiration to start her own company, and even the company name.

Ms Fong expressed deep gratitude to her rescuers. “Mountain climbing has been a real passion of mine for so long. I thank all the people who were involved in bringing me down from the mountain, and of course the wonderful medical staff who are taking care of me here in Kathmandu hospital.”

The weather forecast for the coming week on the mountain predicts stormy weather. Mountaineers are being warned to avoid the area for now.

Emily Plays Travel Blog

Tokyo 2020’s World Wow Web!

Tokyo, 23rd July 2021

I looked up into the sky last night in Tokyo and saw something strange. Space invaders? Northern lights? Spirits taking flight?

None of the above. So, what was above me? Drones. Nearly 2000 of them, lighting up the night sky like a giant spider web as part of Tokyo’s incredible Olympic Games Opening Ceremony.

I felt extraordinarily lucky to witness the spectacle with Peter O’Rourke, the CEO of Flashbangs, Asia’s leading fireworks company. We were both stunned into silence by the magnificent drone display.

Mr O’Rourke is a leading expert on outdoor lighting displays. I couldn’t resist asking him if what we were seeing represented the future of this industry.

His reply? “I don’t think so.”

Oh really, Mr O’Rourke? That didn’t seem likely.

“You try programming thousands of drones to perform at once!” he snapped. I was worried I’d overstepped the mark... “It’s complicated.”

And expensive, too? He nodded in agreement. It seems traditional lighting displays are safe from drones for now.

We made up over a sushi buffet and some very, very nice sencha tea.

Extracts from Pris Fong’s Diary

7th October 1980

- Lucky escape on the mountain last night. I’m in agony! Not sure how I will get down. Camping here overnight, but it’s freezing. All we have is sour tea and a small fire to keep warm. I hope this won’t be my last diary entry.

8th October 1980

- One of the longest days of my life! Never felt so much pain for so long, but we made it down. I am indebted to my guide for carrying me. What an incredible woman she is! A true hero. She risked her life to save me when I thought all hope was lost. I’m truly grateful just to be alive.

9th October 1980

- Flown to Kathmandu hospital by helicopter today. Incredible view of Manaslu as we left. Made this sketch of what the icy peak looked like in the sun. Unforgettable!

[Image: sketch of an icy mountain peak in the sun, with a diamond-like symbol above it.]

10th October 1980

- Doctors confirmed today the ankle is badly sprained. Believe it or not, I feel lucky that’s all! I’ll be off my feet for a while, the doctors say. I’ve been thinking a lot about my future these few days. More of that as I recover, I suppose.

11th October 1980

- I can’t wait to get home to Hong Kong and see everyone, of course, but after that I’m going to see what it takes to live my dream and start my own company — making neon lights! And the company name? I think my sketch says it all!

……..

30th October 1980

- Made it back to Hong Kong! Time to take the first (gentle!) step on my new ‘adventure’. First things first: I need to get some training. Let’s see if I can do this. The journey starts today!

Attachment to the Wan Yuen Estate Residents’ Association Email

Flash! Bang! Wallop!

Hong Kong, 21st March 2015

Harold Tsang got the shock of his life last Wednesday morning when he found his car crushed under a 5-metre-high lighting display.

“I saw this flash outside my window, then heard a terrific bang and wallop. I looked out the window and saw my car totally smashed up with a huge sign sticking out its roof,” said Mr Tsang.

The display, which ironically advertised accident insurance, had come loose and broke off the North Dragon Gardens rooftop. No one was hurt.

Rick Lai, on behalf of Shiny Diamond Lighting Ltd - the company which installed the display, stated that the early theory about the accident was that it was due to poor maintenance.

Eddie Bong, the leader of an independent team of engineers investigating the incident, later confirmed this, stating that the original installation had passed all safety checks.

“We’re all lucky that no one was hurt,” said local resident Carol Bodega. “Anyone could have been crushed under the display. Terrible!”

North Dragon Gardens building management declined to give any comment.

Meeting Minutes of the Shiny Diamond Management Committee

Friday 22nd April 2022, 2pm
Main Conference Room

In attendance:
Ms Pris Fong
Mr Bati Khatoon (Installation Team Leader)
Mr Rick Lai (Chief Designer)
Mr Floyd Mann (General Manager)

Item 1 — Response to the Wan Yuen Estate Residents’ Association

Ms Fong asked Mr Khatoon whether the concerns expressed about the Fortune Phoenix Towers display by the Wan Yuen Estate Residents’ Association had any merit.

Mr Khatoon pointed out that Shiny Diamond seemed to have been unfairly blamed for something which was not our fault and, quite frankly, out of our control. He asked if the Association had not realised that by law all outdoor lighting displays had to be dimmed by the building’s management by 11pm, among other relevant regulations.

Mr Lai said that since the incident Shiny Diamond now worked closely with each building’s management office to ensure regular maintenance was carried out.

Ms Fong suggested that these two facts should be highlighted in any response, and that Shiny Diamond should offer to meet members of the Wan Yuen Estate Residents’ Association. She insisted that it be stressed that the company cared deeply about residents living near our lighting displays. She also recommended avoiding Mr Khatoon’s language about “fault” and “blame”.

Item 2 — The commercial use of drone displays

Ms Fong stated that it was important for Shiny Diamond to consider offering drone displays as an alternative to neon. She distributed printed copies of the Emily Plays travel blog for the Committee’s consideration.

Mr Lai agreed with the overall message of the blog, although the display had been impressive.

Mr Khatoon expressed further concerns over drones’ safety issues that meant their use would be limited. When asked, he clarified that they required good weather conditions. Finally, he advised Committee members to view the most recent Customer Survey, which demonstrated the nature of neon lights’ lasting appeal. This should therefore be the continuing focus of the company.

Ms Fong agreed and brought the meeting to a close.
