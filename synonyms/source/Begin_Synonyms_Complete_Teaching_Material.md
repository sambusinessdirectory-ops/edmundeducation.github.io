# BEGIN — Complete Synonym Teaching Material

## Overview

**begin** = 開始；開始進行；開始發生

“Begin” is a broad verb. English often chooses a more precise word depending on **what is starting**, **who causes it to start**, **how formal the situation is**, and **whether the beginning is gradual, sudden, official, or deliberate**.

This lesson uses **18 useful alternatives and near-synonyms**. They are not completely interchangeable.

---

# 01 Synonym Guide

## 01. Start

**Meaning:** 開始；著手  
**Core idea:** The most common everyday alternative to **begin**.

**Usage note:**  
Very broad and natural in conversation. It can be transitive or intransitive.

**Common patterns:**
- start a meeting
- start work
- start doing something
- start to do something
- the film starts at 8

**Example:**  
The meeting will **start** at nine.

**中文:**  
會議將於九時開始。

**Contrast:**  
**start** is usually more conversational than **commence**.

---

## 02. Commence

**Meaning:** 開始；著手進行  
**Core idea:** A formal alternative to **begin**.

**Usage note:**  
Common in official notices, ceremonies, legal writing, formal announcements, and institutional contexts.

**Common patterns:**
- commence work
- commence operations
- commence proceedings
- commence at 10 a.m.

**Example:**  
Construction will **commence** in early May.

**中文:**  
建築工程將於五月初開始。

**Contrast:**  
Use **start** in ordinary conversation; use **commence** when the tone is formal or official.

---

## 03. Initiate

**Meaning:** 發起；啟動  
**Core idea:** Deliberately cause a process, plan, discussion, or procedure to begin.

**Usage note:**  
Usually transitive: someone **initiates something**.

**Common patterns:**
- initiate a discussion
- initiate an investigation
- initiate contact
- initiate a process

**Example:**  
The committee **initiated** an investigation into the complaint.

**中文:**  
委員會就該投訴展開了調查。

**Contrast:**  
**begin** can simply describe something starting. **Initiate** emphasizes the person or organization that deliberately sets it in motion.

---

## 04. Launch

**Meaning:** 推出；發起；啟動  
**Core idea:** Begin something publicly, energetically, or as a new organized effort.

**Common patterns:**
- launch a product
- launch a campaign
- launch a programme
- launch an investigation
- launch a website

**Example:**  
The company **launched** a new training programme for staff.

**中文:**  
公司推出了一項新的員工培訓計劃。

**Contrast:**  
A project can **begin**, but **launch** suggests an active, often public or organized start.

---

## 05. Embark on

**Meaning:** 開始從事；著手  
**Core idea:** Begin a substantial activity, journey, course, or long-term undertaking.

**Usage note:**  
Often used for something important, demanding, or extended.

**Common patterns:**
- embark on a journey
- embark on a career
- embark on a project
- embark on a reform programme

**Example:**  
She **embarked on** a new career in medicine at the age of forty.

**中文:**  
她四十歲時開始了新的醫療事業。

**Contrast:**  
**Embark on** is stronger than simply **begin** because it suggests entering into a significant undertaking.

---

## 06. Set about

**Meaning:** 著手做  
**Core idea:** Begin doing something in a practical, purposeful way.

**Pattern:**  
**set about + noun / -ing**

**Example:**  
They **set about repairing** the damaged roof immediately.

**中文:**  
他們立即著手修理受損的屋頂。

**Contrast:**  
**Set about** emphasizes getting to work on the task.

---

## 07. Set out

**Meaning:** 開始做；著手達成  
**Core idea:** Begin with a clear intention or goal.

**Pattern:**  
**set out to + verb**

**Example:**  
The researchers **set out to discover** why the disease was spreading.

**中文:**  
研究人員著手查明疾病擴散的原因。

**Contrast:**  
**Set out to** emphasizes purpose: you begin because you intend to achieve something.

---

## 08. Open

**Meaning:** 開始；揭開序幕  
**Core idea:** Begin an event, meeting, performance, speech, discussion, or period.

**Common patterns:**
- open a meeting
- open a conference
- open the debate
- the play opens with...
- open the session

**Example:**  
The chairperson **opened** the meeting with a short welcome.

**中文:**  
主席以簡短的歡迎詞開始會議。

**Contrast:**  
**Open** is especially natural when something has a recognizable formal beginning.

---

## 09. Kick off

**Meaning:** 開始；揭開序幕  
**Core idea:** An informal, energetic way to say that an event or activity begins.

**Usage note:**  
Informal. Common in conversation, business, sports, events, and media.

**Example:**  
We will **kick off** the workshop with a short group activity.

**中文:**  
我們會以一個簡短的小組活動開始工作坊。

**Contrast:**  
**Kick off** is much less formal than **commence**.

---

## 10. Get underway

**Meaning:** 開始進行；正式展開  
**Core idea:** An activity or event moves from preparation into actual progress.

**Usage note:**  
Usually intransitive.

**Example:**  
The rescue operation **got underway** shortly after sunrise.

**中文:**  
救援行動在日出後不久正式展開。

**Contrast:**  
**Begin** marks the start. **Get underway** often suggests that real activity is now in progress.

---

## 11. Originate

**Meaning:** 起源於；開始於  
**Core idea:** Have a particular source, place, cause, or historical beginning.

**Common patterns:**
- originate in
- originate from
- the idea originated with...

**Example:**  
The custom **originated** in northern Europe.

**中文:**  
這個習俗起源於北歐。

**Contrast:**  
Use **originate** when discussing where something first came from, not simply when an activity starts.

---

## 12. Arise

**Meaning:** 出現；產生  
**Core idea:** A problem, need, question, difficulty, or situation begins to exist.

**Usage note:**  
Intransitive.

**Common patterns:**
- a problem arises
- a need arises
- a question arises
- difficulties arise

**Example:**  
Several problems **arose** during the final stage of the project.

**中文:**  
項目最後階段出現了幾個問題。

**Contrast:**  
We usually do not say someone “arises a problem.” The problem itself **arises**.

---

## 13. Emerge

**Meaning:** 出現；逐漸顯現  
**Core idea:** Begin to appear, become known, or become visible.

**Common patterns:**
- a pattern emerges
- evidence emerges
- a leader emerges
- details emerge

**Example:**  
A clear pattern **began to emerge** from the data.

**中文:**  
數據中開始逐漸顯現出一個清晰的模式。

**Contrast:**  
**Emerge** often suggests that something becomes noticeable gradually.

---

## 14. Dawn

**Meaning:** 開始被理解；開始出現  
**Core idea:** A realization or a new period begins.

**Common patterns:**
- it dawned on someone that...
- a new era dawned
- the day dawned

**Example:**  
It slowly **dawned on** him that he had misunderstood the instructions.

**中文:**  
他慢慢開始意識到自己誤解了指示。

**Contrast:**  
This is not a general replacement for **begin**. It is mainly used for realizations, days, or metaphorical new eras.

---

## 15. Break out

**Meaning:** 爆發；突然開始  
**Core idea:** Something undesirable or uncontrolled begins suddenly.

**Common patterns:**
- a fire breaks out
- war breaks out
- violence breaks out
- a fight breaks out

**Example:**  
A fire **broke out** in the warehouse during the night.

**中文:**  
倉庫在夜間發生火災。

**Contrast:**  
**Break out** is used for sudden events such as fire, war, disease, fighting, or violence—not ordinary planned activities.

---

## 16. Erupt

**Meaning:** 爆發；突然發生  
**Core idea:** Begin suddenly and violently or emotionally.

**Common patterns:**
- violence erupts
- protests erupt
- laughter erupts
- an argument erupts
- a volcano erupts

**Example:**  
Violence **erupted** after the announcement.

**中文:**  
公告發出後爆發了暴力事件。

**Contrast:**  
**Erupt** is more dramatic than **begin** and usually implies sudden intensity.

---

## 17. Take up

**Meaning:** 開始從事；開始培養  
**Core idea:** Begin a new activity, hobby, subject, job, or position.

**Common patterns:**
- take up painting
- take up a sport
- take up a post
- take up a new hobby

**Example:**  
He **took up** photography after retiring.

**中文:**  
他退休後開始學攝影。

**Contrast:**  
**Take up** is especially natural for hobbies, activities, interests, and roles.

---

## 18. Enter into

**Meaning:** 開始進入；開始建立  
**Core idea:** Begin a formal relationship, agreement, discussion, or arrangement.

**Common patterns:**
- enter into negotiations
- enter into an agreement
- enter into a contract
- enter into a partnership

**Example:**  
The two companies **entered into** negotiations over the proposed merger.

**中文:**  
兩家公司就擬議中的合併開始進行談判。

**Contrast:**  
**Enter into** is usually used for formal relationships, agreements, negotiations, or arrangements.

# 03 False Synonyms and Common Traps

## 01. Begin / Become

**Trap:**  
**begin** means “start.”  
**become** means “change into a new state.”

**Compare:**
- It **began** to rain. = 下雨開始了。
- It **became** cold. = 天氣變冷了。

Do not use **become** simply because something changes at the beginning of a process.

---

## 02. Begin / Create

**Trap:**  
To **create** something is to make it exist. To **begin** something is to start it.

**Compare:**
- They **began** the project in March.
- They **created** a new software tool.

Something can be created and then begin operating later.

---

## 03. Begin / Prepare

**Trap:**  
Preparation happens **before** the main activity begins.

**Compare:**
- We **prepared for** the meeting at 8:30.
- The meeting **began** at 9:00.

---

## 04. Begin / Introduce

**Trap:**  
**Introduce** can mean present something new, but it does not always mean the activity itself begins.

**Compare:**
- The school **introduced** a new policy.
- The policy **came into effect** in September.

---

## 05. Begin / Continue

These are opposites in many contexts.

- **begin** = start
- **continue** = keep going after the start

---

## 06. Initiate / Originate

**initiate** = deliberately cause something to begin.  
**originate** = be the source or first point of something.

**Compare:**
- The manager **initiated** the review.
- The idea **originated** in the design team.

---

## 07. Launch / Start

Both may mean “begin,” but **launch** usually implies a planned, organized, and often public beginning.

- **start a car**
- **start work**
- **launch a product**
- **launch a campaign**

“Launch the car” normally means physically send it forward or introduce a model, not simply turn on the engine.

---

## 08. Arise / Raise

These are different verbs.

- **arise** = appear / come into existence  
- **raise** = lift something or cause something to increase

**Compare:**
- A problem **arose**.
- She **raised** a question.
- The company **raised** prices.

---

## 09. Emerge / Launch

**emerge** often happens gradually or without one clear agent.  
**launch** is deliberate.

**Compare:**
- New evidence **emerged**.
- Police **launched** an investigation.

---

## 10. Break out / Kick off

Both can describe a beginning, but their tone and context are very different.

- The conference **kicked off** at nine.
- A fight **broke out** after the game.

Do not say a normal conference “broke out.”

---

## 11. Set about / Set out

**set about + noun / -ing**  
= start working on something.

**set out to + verb**  
= begin with the intention of achieving something.

**Compare:**
- She **set about writing** the report.
- She **set out to prove** the theory wrong.

---

## 12. Start / Take up

**start** is very broad.  
**take up** is especially natural when beginning a hobby, activity, subject, or role.

- start exercising
- take up yoga
- take up painting
- take up a new post

---

# 04 Grammar and Pattern Guide

## Begin / Start

Both can be followed by:

- **noun**
  - begin the lesson
  - start the lesson

- **to-infinitive**
  - begin to speak
  - start to speak

- **-ing**
  - begin speaking
  - start speaking

### Small nuance

In many contexts, **begin to do** and **begin doing** are both correct.

Example:
- It began to rain.
- It began raining.

Both are natural.

---

## Commence

Often followed by a noun:

- commence work
- commence proceedings
- commence operations

It can also be intransitive:

- The ceremony commenced at noon.

---

## Initiate

Usually takes an object:

- initiate a review
- initiate contact
- initiate proceedings

Not usually:
- “The review initiated.” ✗

Better:
- The review **began**.
- The committee **initiated** the review.

---

## Embark on

Pattern:

**embark on + noun / noun phrase**

- embark on a journey
- embark on a project
- embark on a career

Not:
- embark to study ✗

Better:
- embark on a course of study
- begin to study

---

## Set about

Pattern:

**set about + noun / -ing**

- set about the task
- set about solving the problem

---

## Set out

Pattern:

**set out to + base verb**

- set out to improve
- set out to investigate
- set out to build

---

## Get underway

Usually no object:

- The project got underway.
- The talks got underway.

Not:
- “They got underway the project.” ✗

---

## Arise

Intransitive:

- a problem arises
- difficulties arise

No direct object.

---

## Take up

Pattern:

**take up + noun / -ing-like activity noun**

- take up tennis
- take up painting
- take up a position

---

## Exercise 01.1 — Start

**Target word:** start

**Context:**  
The lesson will begin at ten o'clock.

**Options:**  
A. arise  
B. start  
C. originate  
D. erupt  
E. initiate  
F. emerge

**Correct answer:** **B. start**

**Rewritten sentence:**  
The lesson will **start** at ten o'clock.

**Why:**  
**Start** is the natural everyday verb for a normal scheduled beginning.

**Option feedback:**
- **A. arise** — used when a problem, question, or need appears.
- **B. start** — correct; neutral and natural.
- **C. originate** — means come from a source.
- **D. erupt** — suggests sudden, intense activity.
- **E. initiate** — normally requires someone deliberately starting a process.
- **F. emerge** — means gradually appear or become known.

# 06 Contrast Sets

## Start vs Commence

- **start** = everyday, neutral
- **commence** = formal, official

Examples:
- The class **starts** at nine.
- Formal proceedings **commence** at nine.

---

## Begin vs Initiate

- **begin** = something starts
- **initiate** = someone deliberately causes it to start

Examples:
- The investigation **began** on Monday.
- Police **initiated** the investigation on Monday.

---

## Start vs Launch

- **start** = broad
- **launch** = begin an organized/public effort

Examples:
- start work
- start the engine
- launch a campaign
- launch a product

---

## Embark on vs Set about

- **embark on** = enter a substantial undertaking
- **set about** = get to work on a particular task

Examples:
- embark on a new career
- set about repairing the roof

---

## Set about vs Set out to

- **set about + -ing**
- **set out to + verb**

Examples:
- She set about **writing** the report.
- She set out **to explain** the problem.

---

## Arise vs Emerge

- **arise** = come into existence, especially a problem, need, or question
- **emerge** = become visible, known, or noticeable

Examples:
- A problem arose.
- New evidence emerged.

---

## Break out vs Erupt

Both can describe sudden beginnings.

**break out** commonly collocates with:
- fire
- war
- fighting
- disease
- violence

**erupt** emphasizes sudden intensity:
- violence erupted
- protests erupted
- laughter erupted
- an argument erupted

---

## Take up vs Embark on

- **take up** = begin a hobby, interest, activity, or role
- **embark on** = begin a major undertaking, journey, career, or programme

Examples:
- take up golf
- take up painting
- embark on a career
- embark on a major reform programme

# 09 Core Learning Principle

Do not replace **begin** mechanically.

Ask:

1. Is the beginning **ordinary**? → **start**
2. Is it **formal or official**? → **commence**
3. Is someone deliberately causing a process to start? → **initiate**
4. Is it a public or organized effort? → **launch**
5. Is it a substantial undertaking? → **embark on**
6. Is someone getting to work on a task? → **set about**
7. Is there a clear goal? → **set out to**
8. Is an event/meeting being formally opened? → **open**
9. Is the tone informal and energetic? → **kick off**
10. Is the activity moving into real progress? → **get underway**
11. Are you describing the historical/source beginning? → **originate**
12. Is a problem, need, or question appearing? → **arise**
13. Is something gradually becoming visible or known? → **emerge**
14. Is it a realization or new era? → **dawn**
15. Is fire, war, fighting, or similar trouble suddenly starting? → **break out**
16. Is the beginning sudden and intense? → **erupt**
17. Is someone beginning a hobby, activity, or role? → **take up**
18. Is a formal agreement, partnership, or negotiation beginning? → **enter into**
