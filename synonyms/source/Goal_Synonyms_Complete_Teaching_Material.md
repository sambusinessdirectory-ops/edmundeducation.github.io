# GOAL — Complete Synonym Teaching Material

## Overview

**goal** = 目標；目的；想要達成的結果

“Goal” is a broad noun. English chooses different words depending on whether the goal is:

- a **specific measurable result**
- a general **aim or purpose**
- a personal **dream**
- a strong long-term **ambition**
- an idealistic **aspiration**
- an official **objective**
- a concrete **target**
- a life or organizational **mission**
- an intended **end result**
- an intermediate **milestone**

This lesson uses **18 useful alternatives and near-synonyms**. They overlap, but they are not completely interchangeable.

A particularly important distinction is:

**dream → ambition → aspiration**

These all relate to hopes for the future, but the tone and strength differ.

---

# 01 Synonym Guide

## 01. Aim

**Meaning:** 目標；目的  
**Core idea:** A general result that someone wants to achieve.

**Common patterns:**
- main aim
- long-term aim
- aim of the project
- achieve an aim

**Example:**  
The main **aim** of the programme is to improve literacy.

**中文:**  
這個計劃的主要目標是提高識字能力。

**Contrast:**  
**Aim** is broad and natural. It is often less formal and less measurable than **objective**.

---

## 02. Objective

**Meaning:** 具體目標；目的  
**Core idea:** A clearly defined result, often in business, education, policy, research, or planning.

**Common patterns:**
- project objective
- learning objective
- strategic objective
- meet an objective
- achieve an objective

**Example:**  
One of our main **objectives** is to reduce waiting times by 20 percent.

**中文:**  
我們其中一個主要目標是將等候時間縮短百分之二十。

**Contrast:**  
An **objective** is usually more precise and formal than a general **goal** or **aim**.

---

## 03. Target

**Meaning:** 目標；指標  
**Core idea:** A specific result, number, level, or deadline that someone is trying to reach.

**Common patterns:**
- sales target
- emissions target
- target weight
- meet a target
- hit a target

**Example:**  
The company met its annual sales **target** ahead of schedule.

**中文:**  
公司提前達到了年度銷售目標。

**Contrast:**  
**Target** is especially natural when the goal can be measured.

---

## 04. Purpose

**Meaning:** 目的；用途  
**Core idea:** The reason why something is done or exists.

**Common patterns:**
- main purpose
- purpose of the meeting
- serve a purpose
- for the purpose of

**Example:**  
The **purpose** of the meeting is to agree on the next steps.

**中文:**  
這次會議的目的是就下一步行動達成共識。

**Contrast:**  
**Goal** asks “What do you want to achieve?”  
**Purpose** asks “Why are you doing this?”

---

## 05. Intention

**Meaning:** 意圖；打算  
**Core idea:** Something a person plans or means to do.

**Common patterns:**
- intention to do something
- have no intention of
- original intention
- clear intention

**Example:**  
Her **intention** is to return to university next year.

**中文:**  
她打算明年重返大學。

**Contrast:**  
An **intention** is a plan in the mind. A **goal** is the result you want to achieve.

---

## 06. Ambition

**Meaning:** 抱負；雄心；志向  
**Core idea:** A strong desire to achieve something important, difficult, or prestigious.

**Common patterns:**
- lifelong ambition
- political ambition
- career ambition
- fulfil an ambition
- ambition to become...

**Example:**  
Her lifelong **ambition** is to become a surgeon.

**中文:**  
她一生的志向是成為外科醫生。

**Contrast:**  
**Ambition** usually suggests determination and achievement, often in career or status.

---

## 07. Aspiration

**Meaning:** 抱負；理想；渴望達成的目標  
**Core idea:** A strong hope to achieve something worthwhile, often personal, idealistic, or long-term.

**Common patterns:**
- career aspirations
- educational aspirations
- personal aspirations
- high aspirations
- fulfil one's aspirations

**Example:**  
The programme helps young people pursue their educational **aspirations**.

**中文:**  
這個計劃幫助年輕人追求教育方面的理想。

**Contrast:**  
**Aspiration** often sounds more idealistic and elevated than **ambition**.

---

## 08. Dream

**Meaning:** 夢想；理想  
**Core idea:** Something greatly desired, often emotionally important but not necessarily planned in detail.

**Common patterns:**
- childhood dream
- lifelong dream
- dream of becoming...
- fulfil a dream
- pursue a dream

**Example:**  
It was his childhood **dream** to become a pilot.

**中文:**  
成為飛行員是他兒時的夢想。

**Contrast:**  
A **dream** can exist without a practical plan. An **ambition** usually suggests stronger determination to achieve it.

---

## 09. Mission

**Meaning:** 使命；重大目標  
**Core idea:** A purpose or goal that gives strong direction to a person or organization.

**Common patterns:**
- company mission
- personal mission
- mission to improve...
- on a mission to...

**Example:**  
The charity's **mission** is to provide clean water to rural communities.

**中文:**  
這個慈善機構的使命是為農村社區提供清潔用水。

**Contrast:**  
A **mission** is usually broader and more identity-defining than a single goal.

---

## 10. End

**Meaning:** 目的；最終目標  
**Core idea:** The final result or purpose being pursued.

**Common patterns:**
- to this end
- means to an end
- achieve one's ends

**Example:**  
Education is not merely a means to an **end**.

**中文:**  
教育不只是一種達成目的的手段。

**Contrast:**  
This sense of **end** is more formal and abstract than ordinary **goal**.

---

## 11. Desired outcome

**Meaning:** 期望結果  
**Core idea:** The result that someone hopes a process, plan, or action will produce.

**Common patterns:**
- desired outcome
- expected outcome
- achieve the desired outcome

**Example:**  
Clear communication is essential if we want to achieve the **desired outcome**.

**中文:**  
如果我們想達到期望的結果，清晰溝通是必要的。

**Contrast:**  
An **outcome** is a result. A **desired outcome** is the result you want.

---

## 12. Milestone

**Meaning:** 里程碑；階段性目標  
**Core idea:** An important point reached on the way toward a larger goal.

**Common patterns:**
- major milestone
- key milestone
- reach a milestone
- project milestone

**Example:**  
Completing the first prototype was an important **milestone**.

**中文:**  
完成第一個原型是一個重要的里程碑。

**Contrast:**  
A **milestone** is not usually the final goal; it marks progress toward it.

---

## 13. Priority

**Meaning:** 優先事項；首要目標  
**Core idea:** Something that should receive attention before other things.

**Common patterns:**
- top priority
- main priority
- make something a priority
- immediate priority

**Example:**  
Improving patient safety is our top **priority**.

**中文:**  
改善病人安全是我們的首要事項。

**Contrast:**  
A **priority** is about order of importance, not necessarily the final result.

---

## 14. Ideal

**Meaning:** 理想；理想目標  
**Core idea:** A standard or condition regarded as perfect or highly desirable.

**Common patterns:**
- political ideal
- personal ideal
- pursue an ideal
- ideal of equality

**Example:**  
They continued to work toward the **ideal** of equal access to education.

**中文:**  
他們繼續努力追求教育機會平等的理想。

**Contrast:**  
An **ideal** is often more abstract and value-based than a practical goal.

---

## 15. Desire

**Meaning:** 願望；渴望  
**Core idea:** Something someone strongly wants.

**Common patterns:**
- strong desire
- desire to succeed
- desire for change
- fulfil a desire

**Example:**  
His **desire** to help others led him to study medicine.

**中文:**  
他幫助別人的願望令他選擇讀醫。

**Contrast:**  
A **desire** is a feeling of wanting; a **goal** usually implies a result being pursued.

---

## 16. Hope

**Meaning:** 希望；期望  
**Core idea:** Something someone wants to happen, especially when the outcome is uncertain.

**Common patterns:**
- hope of doing something
- hope for the future
- in the hope that...
- fulfil one's hopes

**Example:**  
Her greatest **hope** is that her children will have more opportunities.

**中文:**  
她最大的希望是孩子們能有更多機會。

**Contrast:**  
**Hope** does not necessarily imply control or a concrete plan.

---

## 17. Resolution

**Meaning:** 決心；立下的目標  
**Core idea:** A firm decision to change behavior or achieve something.

**Common patterns:**
- New Year's resolution
- make a resolution
- keep a resolution
- resolution to exercise more

**Example:**  
Her New Year's **resolution** is to exercise three times a week.

**中文:**  
她的新年目標是每星期運動三次。

**Contrast:**  
A **resolution** is a commitment or decision, not just a desired result.

---

## 18. Quest

**Meaning:** 追求；探索；長期追尋的目標  
**Core idea:** A determined, often long or difficult search for something important.

**Common patterns:**
- quest for knowledge
- quest for justice
- quest for success
- lifelong quest

**Example:**  
His research became a lifelong **quest** for a cure.

**中文:**  
他的研究成為一生尋找治療方法的追求。

**Contrast:**  
A **quest** emphasizes the journey of pursuit, not just the final goal.

# 02 The Key Chain: Dream → Ambition → Aspiration

These three words are closely related, but they are not identical.

## Dream

**Dream** is the broadest and most emotional.

It expresses something a person would love to achieve.

It may be:
- realistic
- unrealistic
- carefully planned
- not planned at all

**Example:**  
My **dream** is to live by the sea.

**中文:**  
我的夢想是住在海邊。

A dream can remain only a dream.

---

## Ambition

**Ambition** usually adds **determination and achievement**.

It often relates to:
- career
- success
- leadership
- status
- difficult accomplishments

**Example:**  
His **ambition** is to lead his own research team.

**中文:**  
他的志向是領導自己的研究團隊。

### Core difference

**dream** = what you would love  
**ambition** = what you strongly intend to achieve

---

## Aspiration

**Aspiration** often sounds more **idealistic, elevated, or values-based**.

It is common in:
- education
- career development
- social mobility
- personal growth
- public policy

**Example:**  
The scholarship enables students from low-income families to pursue their **aspirations**.

**中文:**  
這項獎學金讓低收入家庭的學生能追求自己的理想。

### Core difference

**ambition** often emphasizes **achievement**.  
**aspiration** often emphasizes **becoming, improving, or reaching an ideal**.

---

## Side-by-side

| Word | Main feeling | Planning implied? | Typical contexts |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **dream** | emotional hope | not necessarily | childhood, lifestyle, personal hopes |
| **ambition** | determination to achieve | often yes | career, success, leadership |
| **aspiration** | elevated hope or ideal | sometimes | education, growth, social progress |

# 04 False Synonyms and Common Traps

## 01. Goal / Purpose

**goal** = the result you want to achieve  
**purpose** = the reason you are doing something

**Compare:**
- Our **goal** is to reduce errors by 30%.
- The **purpose** of the new system is to improve safety.

---

## 02. Goal / Target

A **target** is usually more measurable.

**Compare:**
- goal: improve customer satisfaction
- target: achieve a satisfaction score of 90%

A target is often one concrete expression of a broader goal.

---

## 03. Goal / Objective

**objective** is more formal and specific.

**Compare:**
- general goal: improve English
- learning objective: use ten new academic words correctly

---

## 04. Dream / Plan

A **dream** is something desired.  
A **plan** explains how you intend to achieve something.

**Compare:**
- My dream is to open a café.
- My plan is to save $20,000 and open one within three years.

---

## 05. Ambition / Greed

**ambition** does not automatically mean greed.

A person can be ambitious about:
- becoming a doctor
- helping a community
- mastering a skill
- building a business

**Greed** specifically involves excessive desire, especially for money or possessions.

---

## 06. Aspiration / Inspiration

These look similar but are different.

**aspiration** = a hope or ambition  
**inspiration** = something that motivates or gives ideas

**Compare:**
- Her aspiration is to become a teacher.
- Her mother was her inspiration.

---

## 07. Intention / Goal

**intention** = what you mean or plan to do  
**goal** = the result you want

**Compare:**
- I intend to study every evening.
- My goal is to pass the exam.

---

## 08. Hope / Expectation

**hope** = what you want to happen  
**expectation** = what you think is likely to happen

**Compare:**
- I hope we win.
- I expect the match to be close.

You can hope for something you do not expect.

---

## 09. Milestone / Goal

A **milestone** is usually a stage on the way.

**Example:**
- final goal: become fluent
- milestone: hold a 10-minute conversation comfortably

---

## 10. Priority / Objective

A **priority** says what should come first.  
An **objective** says what result should be achieved.

**Example:**
- priority: patient safety
- objective: reduce medication errors by 25%

---

## 11. Desire / Determination

**desire** = wanting something  
**determination** = persistence in trying to achieve it

You can strongly desire something without being determined enough to pursue it.

---

## 12. Mission / Task

A **task** is a specific piece of work.  
A **mission** is a broader purpose.

**Compare:**
- task: update the database
- mission: make healthcare information accessible to everyone

---

# 07 Contrast Sets

## Goal vs Aim

Both are broad.

- **goal** = desired result
- **aim** = intended result or direction

Examples:
- My goal is to run a marathon.
- The aim of the programme is to improve access.

---

## Goal vs Objective

- **goal** = broad desired result
- **objective** = specific, formal, clearly defined result

Example:
- goal: improve customer service
- objective: answer 90% of calls within 30 seconds

---

## Goal vs Target

- **goal** = may be broad
- **target** = usually measurable

Example:
- goal: become healthier
- target: walk 8,000 steps per day

---

## Purpose vs Goal

- **purpose** = why
- **goal** = what result

Example:
- Purpose: improve public safety.
- Goal: reduce accidents by 20%.

---

## Dream vs Ambition

- **dream** = emotionally desired future
- **ambition** = strong determination to achieve something

Example:
- childhood dream: become an astronaut
- career ambition: lead a space-research team

---

## Ambition vs Aspiration

- **ambition** = achievement-oriented, often determined and competitive
- **aspiration** = elevated hope connected to growth, ideals, or future potential

Example:
- ambition to become CEO
- aspiration to improve educational opportunities

---

## Dream vs Aspiration

- **dream** sounds personal and emotional
- **aspiration** sounds more formal and elevated

Example:
- my dream of owning a home
- students' educational aspirations

---

## Intention vs Resolution

- **intention** = plan or intended action
- **resolution** = firmer commitment to change

Example:
- I intend to exercise more.
- I made a resolution to exercise three times a week.

---

## Milestone vs Target

- **milestone** = important stage reached
- **target** = specific result to hit

Example:
- target: reach 1,000 users
- milestone: release version 1.0

---

## Mission vs Objective

- **mission** = broad defining purpose
- **objective** = specific result supporting that mission

Example:
- mission: make education accessible
- objective: provide 10,000 free courses this year

# 10 Core Learning Principle

Do not replace **goal** mechanically.

Ask what kind of “goal” is meant:

1. Is it a broad result? → **aim**
2. Is it formal and precisely defined? → **objective**
3. Is it measurable? → **target**
4. Is it the reason for doing something? → **purpose**
5. Is it a planned action? → **intention**
6. Is it a strong achievement-oriented long-term goal? → **ambition**
7. Is it an elevated hope for growth or future achievement? → **aspiration**
8. Is it an emotionally important hope? → **dream**
9. Does it define a person's or organization's role? → **mission**
10. Is it the final purpose? → **end**
11. Is it the result a process should produce? → **desired outcome**
12. Is it an important stage on the way? → **milestone**
13. Is it what should come first? → **priority**
14. Is it an abstract value or perfect standard? → **ideal**
15. Is it simply something strongly wanted? → **desire**
16. Is the result wanted but uncertain or outside full control? → **hope**
17. Is it a firm personal commitment? → **resolution**
18. Is it a long, difficult pursuit? → **quest**
