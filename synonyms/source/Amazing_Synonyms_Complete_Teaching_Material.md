# AMAZING — Complete Synonym Teaching Material

## Overview

**amazing** = 令人驚訝的；令人驚嘆的；非常出色的

“Amazing” is a broad adjective. Depending on context, it may mean:

- causing great **surprise**
- causing **wonder or awe**
- being extremely **good or impressive**
- being visually **beautiful or dramatic**
- being difficult to **believe**
- being unusually **remarkable or exceptional**

English often uses a more precise alternative depending on *why* something is amazing.

This lesson uses **22 useful alternatives and near-synonyms**. They are not completely interchangeable.

---

# 01 Synonym Guide

## 01. Astonishing

**Meaning:** 令人震驚的；令人十分驚訝的  
**Core idea:** So surprising that it is hard to believe at first.

**Common patterns:**
- an astonishing result
- an astonishing discovery
- an astonishing amount
- astonishingly high/low

**Example:**  
The team made an **astonishing** recovery in the second half.

**中文:**  
球隊在下半場出現了令人震驚的反彈。

**Contrast:**  
**Astonishing** emphasizes surprise more strongly than general praise.

---

## 02. Astounding

**Meaning:** 令人震驚的；驚人的  
**Core idea:** Extremely surprising or impressive.

**Common patterns:**
- astounding success
- astounding figures
- astounding speed
- an astounding achievement

**Example:**  
The machine can process data at an **astounding** speed.

**中文:**  
這部機器能以驚人的速度處理數據。

**Contrast:**  
Very close to **astonishing**, often used for scale, speed, numbers, or achievement.

---

## 03. Incredible

**Meaning:** 難以置信的；極好的  
**Core idea:** So surprising, impressive, or good that it seems hard to believe.

**Common patterns:**
- incredible story
- incredible performance
- incredible view
- incredible opportunity

**Example:**  
She gave an **incredible** performance on stage.

**中文:**  
她在舞台上有令人難以置信的精彩演出。

**Contrast:**  
In everyday English, **incredible** can mean either “hard to believe” or simply “extremely good.”

---

## 04. Unbelievable

**Meaning:** 難以置信的；令人難以相信的  
**Core idea:** So surprising or extreme that it seems impossible to believe.

**Common patterns:**
- unbelievable story
- unbelievable price
- unbelievable luck
- unbelievable result

**Example:**  
They won the match in the final second, which was **unbelievable**.

**中文:**  
他們在最後一秒贏得比賽，實在令人難以置信。

**Contrast:**  
**Unbelievable** strongly emphasizes disbelief. It can also be used negatively.

---

## 05. Remarkable

**Meaning:** 非凡的；值得注意的  
**Core idea:** Unusual enough to deserve attention.

**Common patterns:**
- remarkable achievement
- remarkable progress
- remarkable ability
- remarkable improvement

**Example:**  
The patient made a **remarkable** recovery.

**中文:**  
病人的康復情況非常顯著，令人讚嘆。

**Contrast:**  
**Remarkable** is often calmer and more formal than **amazing**.

---

## 06. Extraordinary

**Meaning:** 非凡的；異乎尋常的  
**Core idea:** Far beyond what is ordinary or expected.

**Common patterns:**
- extraordinary talent
- extraordinary circumstances
- extraordinary achievement
- extraordinary beauty

**Example:**  
She showed **extraordinary** courage during the emergency.

**中文:**  
她在緊急情況下表現出非凡的勇氣。

**Contrast:**  
**Extraordinary** emphasizes being outside the ordinary range.

---

## 07. Exceptional

**Meaning:** 卓越的；異常出色的  
**Core idea:** Unusually good or far above the normal standard.

**Common patterns:**
- exceptional quality
- exceptional talent
- exceptional service
- exceptional performance

**Example:**  
The hotel is known for its **exceptional** service.

**中文:**  
這家酒店以卓越的服務聞名。

**Contrast:**  
**Exceptional** is especially useful when comparing quality against a standard.

---

## 08. Phenomenal

**Meaning:** 非凡的；驚人的；極其出色的  
**Core idea:** Extremely impressive, successful, or large in degree.

**Common patterns:**
- phenomenal success
- phenomenal growth
- phenomenal performance
- phenomenal talent

**Example:**  
The new product achieved **phenomenal** sales in its first month.

**中文:**  
這款新產品在首月取得了驚人的銷量。

**Contrast:**  
Often used for unusually large success, growth, ability, or performance.

---

## 09. Spectacular

**Meaning:** 壯觀的；令人驚嘆的  
**Core idea:** Visually dramatic, impressive, or exciting.

**Common patterns:**
- spectacular view
- spectacular scenery
- spectacular display
- spectacular success

**Example:**  
The hotel has a **spectacular** view of the mountains.

**中文:**  
這家酒店擁有壯觀的山景。

**Contrast:**  
**Spectacular** is especially strong for things that create a dramatic visual impression.

---

## 10. Stunning

**Meaning:** 令人驚艷的；極漂亮的；令人震撼的  
**Core idea:** So beautiful or impressive that it has a strong immediate effect.

**Common patterns:**
- stunning view
- stunning dress
- stunning performance
- stunning design

**Example:**  
The sunset over the ocean was **stunning**.

**中文:**  
海上的日落令人驚艷。

**Contrast:**  
Common for beauty, appearance, design, scenery, and highly impressive performances.

---

## 11. Breathtaking

**Meaning:** 美得令人屏息的；驚人的  
**Core idea:** So beautiful, dramatic, or impressive that it figuratively takes your breath away.

**Common patterns:**
- breathtaking scenery
- breathtaking view
- breathtaking beauty
- breathtaking performance

**Example:**  
The view from the summit was **breathtaking**.

**中文:**  
山頂的景色美得令人屏息。

**Contrast:**  
Especially natural for scenery, beauty, and dramatic experiences.

---

## 12. Awe-inspiring

**Meaning:** 令人敬畏的；令人驚嘆的  
**Core idea:** Creating a powerful feeling of awe, wonder, or respect.

**Common patterns:**
- awe-inspiring landscape
- awe-inspiring architecture
- awe-inspiring achievement
- awe-inspiring sight

**Example:**  
The cathedral's interior is truly **awe-inspiring**.

**中文:**  
這座大教堂的內部確實令人敬畏讚嘆。

**Contrast:**  
Stronger than simple admiration; it suggests wonder mixed with respect.

---

## 13. Impressive

**Meaning:** 令人印象深刻的  
**Core idea:** Producing admiration because of quality, skill, size, or achievement.

**Common patterns:**
- impressive performance
- impressive results
- impressive building
- impressive skills

**Example:**  
Her knowledge of the subject is **impressive**.

**中文:**  
她對這個主題的知識令人印象深刻。

**Contrast:**  
**Impressive** does not necessarily mean surprising; something can be impressive even if expected.

---

## 14. Wonderful

**Meaning:** 極好的；令人愉快的；美妙的  
**Core idea:** Very good, enjoyable, or delightful.

**Common patterns:**
- wonderful experience
- wonderful news
- wonderful person
- wonderful meal

**Example:**  
We had a **wonderful** time at the concert.

**中文:**  
我們在音樂會度過了非常愉快的時光。

**Contrast:**  
**Wonderful** focuses more on positive feeling than surprise.

---

## 15. Marvellous / Marvelous

**Meaning:** 極好的；奇妙的  
**Core idea:** Extremely good, pleasing, or impressive.

**Usage note:**  
**Marvellous** is common British spelling; **marvelous** is common American spelling.

**Example:**  
You did a **marvellous** job on the presentation.

**中文:**  
你的簡報做得非常出色。

**Contrast:**  
Often warm and approving rather than strongly surprising.

---

## 16. Fantastic

**Meaning:** 極好的；了不起的  
**Core idea:** Extremely good or enjoyable.

**Common patterns:**
- fantastic idea
- fantastic opportunity
- fantastic meal
- fantastic performance

**Example:**  
That is a **fantastic** idea.

**中文:**  
那是一個很棒的想法。

**Contrast:**  
Very common in everyday praise. It usually means “excellent” rather than literally “surprising.”

---

## 17. Fabulous

**Meaning:** 極好的；絕佳的  
**Core idea:** Extremely good, attractive, or enjoyable.

**Common patterns:**
- fabulous dress
- fabulous holiday
- fabulous meal
- fabulous place

**Example:**  
We had a **fabulous** meal at the restaurant.

**中文:**  
我們在那家餐廳吃了一頓極好的飯。

**Contrast:**  
Informal and enthusiastic; commonly used for lifestyle, appearance, food, and experiences.

---

## 18. Terrific

**Meaning:** 極好的；非常棒的  
**Core idea:** Extremely good or impressive.

**Common patterns:**
- terrific job
- terrific performance
- terrific news
- terrific opportunity

**Example:**  
You did a **terrific** job.

**中文:**  
你做得非常棒。

**Contrast:**  
In modern everyday English, usually positive. Historically it was connected with fear, but that is not the normal meaning here.

---

## 19. Awesome

**Meaning:** 非常棒的；令人敬畏的  
**Core idea:** Extremely good or impressive; in informal English, a very common strong compliment.

**Common patterns:**
- awesome experience
- awesome idea
- awesome view
- awesome job

**Example:**  
The concert was **awesome**.

**中文:**  
那場音樂會非常棒。

**Contrast:**  
Very informal in everyday use. It can also retain its older literal meaning of inspiring awe.

---

## 20. Mind-blowing

**Meaning:** 令人震撼的；令人難以置信的  
**Core idea:** Extremely surprising, impressive, or mentally overwhelming.

**Usage note:**  
Informal.

**Common patterns:**
- mind-blowing experience
- mind-blowing technology
- mind-blowing fact
- mind-blowing performance

**Example:**  
The amount of information stored in the system is **mind-blowing**.

**中文:**  
這個系統所儲存的資訊量令人震撼。

**Contrast:**  
Much more informal and dramatic than **remarkable** or **impressive**.

---

## 21. Miraculous

**Meaning:** 奇蹟般的；不可思議地幸運的  
**Core idea:** So unlikely or extraordinary that it seems like a miracle.

**Common patterns:**
- miraculous recovery
- miraculous escape
- miraculous survival
- miraculous result

**Example:**  
The doctors described his recovery as **miraculous**.

**中文:**  
醫生形容他的康復是奇蹟般的。

**Contrast:**  
Use only when the event seems extraordinarily unlikely—not as a general synonym for “very good.”

---

## 22. Striking

**Meaning:** 引人注目的；顯著的  
**Core idea:** Immediately noticeable because it is unusual, attractive, or strongly contrasted.

**Common patterns:**
- striking difference
- striking similarity
- striking appearance
- striking image

**Example:**  
There is a **striking** difference between the two designs.

**中文:**  
這兩個設計之間有非常顯著的差異。

**Contrast:**  
**Striking** emphasizes immediate noticeability, not necessarily excellence.

# 03 False Synonyms and Common Traps

## 01. Amazing / Surprising

**surprising** = unexpected  
**amazing** = often stronger; may combine surprise with admiration or wonder

**Compare:**
- It was **surprising** that he arrived early.
- His recovery was **amazing**.

Something can be surprising without being impressive.

---

## 02. Amazing / Impressive

**impressive** = deserving admiration  
**amazing** = may involve surprise, wonder, or strong praise

**Compare:**
- Her technical knowledge is **impressive**.
- Her sudden recovery was **amazing**.

An expected high-quality performance can be impressive without being surprising.

---

## 03. Amazing / Beautiful

**beautiful** specifically describes beauty.  
**amazing** is broader.

**Compare:**
- a beautiful garden
- an amazing discovery

A discovery can be amazing without being beautiful.

---

## 04. Amazing / Interesting

**interesting** = holding attention or curiosity  
**amazing** = causing strong surprise, admiration, or wonder

**Compare:**
- an interesting article
- an amazing achievement

Interesting does not automatically mean extraordinary.

---

## 05. Amazing / Shocking

**shocking** often has a negative meaning: causing horror, outrage, or severe surprise.

**Compare:**
- amazing news = often very good
- shocking news = often disturbing or bad

Do not automatically replace positive **amazing** with **shocking**.

---

## 06. Incredible / Unbelievable

Both can mean “hard to believe.”

But in everyday praise:
- **incredible** often sounds clearly positive
- **unbelievable** can be positive, negative, or genuinely doubtful

**Examples:**
- an incredible performance
- unbelievable incompetence
- an unbelievable story

Context matters.

---

## 07. Spectacular / Wonderful

**spectacular** = dramatic, visually impressive, striking  
**wonderful** = very good, enjoyable, delightful

**Compare:**
- spectacular fireworks
- a wonderful friend

“Spectacular friend” is usually unnatural unless describing something unusually dramatic about the person.

---

## 08. Stunning / Astonishing

**stunning** often emphasizes beauty or immediate visual impact.  
**astonishing** emphasizes surprise.

**Compare:**
- a stunning dress
- an astonishing discovery

---

## 09. Remarkable / Exceptional

**remarkable** = noteworthy, unusual  
**exceptional** = far above the normal standard

**Compare:**
- remarkable progress
- exceptional service

There is overlap, but the emphasis differs.

---

## 10. Awesome / Awe-inspiring

Modern informal **awesome** often simply means “excellent.”

**awe-inspiring** retains the stronger literal meaning of producing awe.

**Compare:**
- That pizza was awesome.
- The scale of the universe is awe-inspiring.

---

## 11. Phenomenal / Phenomenon

**phenomenal** = adjective  
**phenomenon** = noun

**Correct:**
- phenomenal growth
- a remarkable phenomenon

**Incorrect:**
- phenomenon growth ✗

---

## 12. Miraculous / Wonderful

**wonderful** = extremely good  
**miraculous** = so unlikely that it seems like a miracle

**Compare:**
- a wonderful holiday
- a miraculous escape

Do not call an ordinary pleasant experience “miraculous” unless deliberately exaggerating.

---

## 13. Terrific / Terrifying

These look related but have very different modern meanings.

- **terrific** = excellent
- **terrifying** = frightening

**Example:**
- a terrific performance
- a terrifying accident

---

## 14. Striking / Stunning

Both can mean visually noticeable.

- **striking** = noticeable, distinctive
- **stunning** = extremely beautiful or impressive

**Example:**
- a striking contrast
- a stunning view

---

# 04 Register and Tone Guide

## Neutral / Formal

Useful in academic, professional, or restrained writing:

- remarkable
- extraordinary
- exceptional
- impressive
- striking
- astonishing
- astounding

**Example:**  
The study produced **remarkable** results.

---

## Strong but Broad

Useful in general speech and writing:

- incredible
- unbelievable
- phenomenal
- spectacular
- stunning
- breathtaking

---

## Warm Positive Praise

Useful for people, experiences, ideas, food, work, and events:

- wonderful
- marvellous / marvelous
- fantastic
- fabulous
- terrific

---

## Informal / Conversational

- awesome
- mind-blowing

These may be unsuitable for very formal academic writing.

---

## Highly Specific

- **miraculous** — extraordinarily unlikely, like a miracle
- **awe-inspiring** — produces awe
- **breathtaking** — beauty/drama that figuratively takes your breath away
- **spectacular** — visually dramatic
- **striking** — immediately noticeable

# 06 Contrast Sets

## Astonishing vs Astounding

Both strongly emphasize surprise.

- **astonishing** = extremely surprising
- **astounding** = extremely surprising/impressive, often in scale or degree

Examples:
- an astonishing discovery
- astounding growth

---

## Incredible vs Unbelievable

Both may mean “hard to believe.”

- **incredible** often works naturally as enthusiastic praise
- **unbelievable** can express praise, criticism, disbelief, or shock

Examples:
- an incredible performance
- unbelievable negligence

---

## Remarkable vs Extraordinary

- **remarkable** = worthy of notice
- **extraordinary** = far outside the ordinary

Examples:
- remarkable progress
- extraordinary courage

---

## Exceptional vs Phenomenal

- **exceptional** = far above normal quality
- **phenomenal** = extremely impressive, successful, or large in scale

Examples:
- exceptional service
- phenomenal growth

---

## Spectacular vs Stunning

- **spectacular** = dramatic and visually impressive
- **stunning** = extremely beautiful or immediately impressive

Examples:
- spectacular fireworks
- a stunning dress

---

## Stunning vs Breathtaking

- **stunning** = extremely attractive or impressive
- **breathtaking** = so beautiful/dramatic that it figuratively takes your breath away

Examples:
- stunning architecture
- breathtaking mountain scenery

---

## Awe-inspiring vs Impressive

- **impressive** = causes admiration
- **awe-inspiring** = causes deep wonder and respect

Examples:
- impressive technical skills
- an awe-inspiring cathedral

---

## Wonderful vs Fantastic

Both are strong positive praise.

- **wonderful** often sounds warm, pleasant, and sincere
- **fantastic** often sounds energetic and enthusiastic

Examples:
- a wonderful friend
- a fantastic opportunity

---

## Fabulous vs Terrific

Both are enthusiastic and informal.

- **fabulous** often works well for appearance, food, places, and experiences
- **terrific** often works well for performance, work, news, and opportunities

---

## Awesome vs Awe-inspiring

- **awesome** = very informal everyday “excellent”
- **awe-inspiring** = literally creates awe

Examples:
- That game was awesome.
- The canyon was awe-inspiring.

---

## Miraculous vs Remarkable

- **remarkable** = notably unusual
- **miraculous** = extraordinarily unlikely, almost like a miracle

Examples:
- remarkable improvement
- miraculous survival

---

## Striking vs Spectacular

- **striking** = immediately noticeable
- **spectacular** = dramatically impressive

Examples:
- a striking difference
- a spectacular display

---

# 09 Core Learning Principle

Do not replace **amazing** mechanically.

Ask what creates the “amazing” feeling:

1. Is it mainly **surprising**? → **astonishing / astounding**
2. Is it hard to **believe**? → **incredible / unbelievable**
3. Is it unusually worthy of **attention**? → **remarkable**
4. Is it far beyond the **ordinary**? → **extraordinary**
5. Is the quality far above the normal **standard**? → **exceptional**
6. Is the scale, growth, success, or performance extreme? → **phenomenal**
7. Is it visually **dramatic**? → **spectacular**
8. Is it immediately **beautiful or impressive**? → **stunning**
9. Is its beauty or drama overwhelming? → **breathtaking**
10. Does it create deep **awe**? → **awe-inspiring**
11. Does it simply deserve **admiration**? → **impressive**
12. Is it mainly very **pleasant or excellent**? → **wonderful / marvellous**
13. Is it enthusiastic everyday praise? → **fantastic / fabulous / terrific**
14. Is the tone very informal? → **awesome / mind-blowing**
15. Does it seem almost like a **miracle**? → **miraculous**
16. Is it especially **noticeable**? → **striking**
